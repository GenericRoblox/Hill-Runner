# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

A 2D physics-based driving platformer ("Hill Climb"-style) built to `SPEC.md`. Plain HTML5/Canvas + vanilla JS ES modules, no build step, no npm. Physics is Matter.js v0.19.0, vendored at `lib/matter.min.js` and loaded as a classic script before the module entry point (exposes global `Matter`).

Scope: Worlds 1–6 (Farm, Town, City, Mines, Castle, Factory) fully playable, 10 levels each; each world unlocks by starring the previous world's final level. Turbo/Gems/ghost-mode/gamepad are deliberate v1 omissions per spec stretch goals.

Look: cartoon UI. Menus are DOM styled by `style.css` (Beachday display font + Fredoka rounded body font, both self-hosted in `font/` — no CDN requests; Fredoka is OFL, license alongside it; note Fredoka has NO ★/☆ glyphs, which is why rating stars are inline SVG via `starIcon()` in ScreenManager.js — never render stars as text), wood/grass boards, toy-block buttons, and a light motion layer (staggered card pop-in, panel bounce, star pop, coin count-up, win confetti — all zeroed by `prefers-reduced-motion`; the result stars use a transition-on-class-add rather than a delayed fill-both animation so a stalled animation clock can never strand them invisible); in-game terrain and most obstacles are filled with tinted tile patterns from `textures/` via `src/ui/Textures.js` (`texPattern(name, multiplyTint, tileSizePx, desat)` — returns null until loaded, callers keep a flat-color fallback; `desat` grayscales before tinting, how the teal `liquid` tile becomes water/lava/acid). Patterns anchor to the current canvas transform — fill MOVING bodies (press blocks, planks, boulders) in translated local coordinates or the texture scrolls through them instead of riding along, and fills made inside a ROTATED context must use `texUpright(name, tint, size, angleRad, desat)` (counter-rotates the tile grid so textures stay upright on screen; plain `texPattern` resets the shared pattern to upright). Per-world fills come from the `tex` entry in `WORLDS`. Backgrounds (sky, parallax, cave ceiling) stay untextured by design. Some source tiles have watermarks/non-seamless edges; `Textures.js` crops them per its `CROP` map.

Debug helpers: `/debug-sim.html?world=4&level=9&tier=t3&from=6` traces one bot run (x/y/vx/vy/grounded per 0.1 s — the fastest way to diagnose a failing level). The game itself takes dev deep-links: `index.html?screen=garage`, or `?world=4&level=2&bot=1&warp=7` (bot holds gas, warp fast-forwards ~7 s — good for `--screenshot` visual checks); add `&veh=sports` to override the vehicle and `&tiers=e,s,t,b` to force its upgrade tiers (e.g. tire-look visuals).

There is no Node.js on this machine — verify JS through the browser (headless Edge as above). No linter or unit-test framework; `test-sim.html` is the test suite.

**Reading sim results:** rows are prefixed F (Farm) / T (Town) / C (City) / M (Mines) / K (Castle/keep) with the vehicle/tier in brackets. Expected green: **Farm passes at STOCK through F9** (F10 stock fails by design — it assumes the 1000-coin engine tier 1, which wins); **Mines passes all 10 at tier 3**; **Castle passes all 10 at maxed**; Town/City mostly pass at tier 1/tier 2 respectively. Known noise (bot-only; humans read the hazard and recover): T4 t1 ends BEACHED perched on a fence top after a bad hop, T5/T10 t1 and T8/T10 t2 miss jumps the next tier clears (T10 maxed wins), T1 sports-stock high-centers on a speed bump and BEACHES (recommended vehicle is the pickup), C8 t2 press timing, C9 t2 (wins t3), C10 below maxed (braking for the wrecking ball drifts the bot into the press corridor), K1/K4 t3 (win maxed), and the Mines MAXED spot-checks M3/M4/M9/M10 are flakier than tier 3 — the extra speed changes every landing. The bot is deliberately naive, so its results are a *lower bound* on playability: a level the bot wins is beatable; one it fails may still be fine for humans — but a level failing at ALL its expected tiers deserves a debug-sim trace (that's how F3/F7's stalled launch lips and M2/M4's hazard-at-landing spacing violations were found and fixed). Rerun after touching physics constants, vehicle stats, level geometry, or obstacles.

## Architecture

**Data-driven core** — `src/data/` defines everything gameplay-numeric:
- `vehicles.js`: roster + 4 linear upgrade tiers per stat (engine/suspension/tires/brakes), resolved by `getStatsAtTiers()` into the flat stat block `Car` consumes
- `levels.js`: Farm + Town + City + Mines levels (10 each) authored via the chainable `LevelBuilder` (y is down; ground baseline y=600; terrain = arrays of point "chains"; `gap()` breaks the chain to create a pit; `pothole()` is a sharp terrain notch; `roof(w)` is a `flat(w)` that also records a building span in `level.buildings`, which `GameScreen._drawBuildings` renders as a facade — cornice, walls, windows — beneath the rooftop). Obstacle set-pieces (`ramp()`, `seesaw()`, `ropeBridge()`, `mudDip()`, `speedBumps()`, `tree()`, the City set: `oilSlick()`, `wreckingBall()`, `press()`, `fan()`, `water()`, `craneLift()`, and the Mines set: `rockfallPit()`, `crumbleBridge()`, `moltenPit()`, `icePatch()`, `jumpHole()`, `tireStack()`, and the Castle set: `fireballPit()`, `spikeStrip()`, `beam()`, `arrowVolley()`, `spikyBall()`) register defs that `physics/Obstacles.js` turns into bodies per run — ramps/seesaws/bumps/oil slicks/presses/tire stacks/spike strips/beams/arrow volleys need flat ground laid beneath them, `ropeBridge()`/`water()`/`craneLift()`/`rockfallPit()`/`crumbleBridge()`/`moltenPit()`/`fireballPit()`/`jumpHole()` cut their own pits, `tree()` trunks are background art with a sensor canopy overhead. `craneLift()` also drops a far-shaft wall so the car stays aboard; press blocks, lift platforms, rockfall boulders, fireballs, arrow-volley phases and crumble-plank releases are driven from a Matter `beforeUpdate` hook inside `Obstacles` (works in game and test-sim alike; lifts rise only while ridden, via `plugin.lastRider`; crumble planks go dynamic ~0.45 s after `plugin.lastRider` is first stamped; rockfall boulders and fireballs cycle parked → in flight → reparked on `period`/`phase`)
- `economy.js`: star and payout rules

Adding a level or vehicle is data-only; adding a world means a new `WORLDS` entry with `playable: true` (plus `sky`/`groundColor`/`grassColor`/`parallax`/`tex`; `cave: true` draws the screen-space tunnel-roof backdrop in `GameScreen._drawCaveCeiling`).

**In-level feel layer (GameScreen, render-only — never touches physics):** the terrain is drawn with flat "diorama aprons" extending 2600 px past both ends of the playfield (synthesized per frame in `_drawTerrain`, because infinite mode's chain list is live); drifting flat-bottomed clouds fill open daytime skies (`_drawClouds`, gated off for cave/factory/castle worlds); the finish flag waves; hard landings kick a dust poof (`ParticleSystem.emitLandingDust`); and full airborne rotations raise a world-space "Flip!" popup (`_popup`/`_drawPopups`, aged in `_updateFx` alongside particles — InfiniteGameScreen's popup shows the coins the flip banked, since flips pay out there).

**Screens** — `src/core/ScreenManager.js` swaps screen objects (`enter/exit/update/render`) via `screens.show(name, params)`. Menus (Home, WorldSelect, LevelSelect, Garage, Upgrade, CustomLevels) are DOM built into `#menu-root` with the `el()` helper; screens that render to canvas (`GameScreen`, `EditorScreen`) declare `usesCanvas: true`, which is what toggles the canvas/menu-root visibility and gates `screens.render` in `main.js`. `InputManager` unifies keyboard + multi-touch into a single Gas/Brake state.

**Menu structure & endgame reveals** — Home is deliberately two big front-door actions (`bigButton()` in HomeScreen: **Play** → WorldSelect, **Garage**) plus the mute toggle; Infinite and Created Levels are NOT on Home. WorldSelect shows the six world cards, then a `.section-head` "Extra Modes" divider, then two gated cards: **Infinite** (`isInfiniteModeUnlocked()` = every level ≥1★) and **Created Levels** (`isCreatorUnlocked()` = every level 3★). Each card renders unlocked only once `qualifies && *Seen` (the `infiniteSeen`/`creatorSeen` save flags); the first time a mode qualifies-but-unseen, WorldSelect.enter() fires `playUnlockCutscene()` (`src/ui/Cutscene.js` — a dim overlay with confetti, a lock-shatter reveal per mode, and a Continue button), marks the flag(s) seen, then re-renders with the card(s) unlocked. Beating the final campaign level (`isFinalCampaignLevel()` in levels.js) that newly unlocks a mode routes there with fanfare: GameScreen `_win()` passes `hideRetry` + `unlockMode` + `quitLabel: 'Unlock New Mode'` to `showResult`, and that exit quits to `worldselect` where the cutscene plays. LevelSelect draws a themed, **live-canvas** backdrop behind the cards per world (`WorldScene` in `src/ui/MenuScenes.js`): a compact level authored with the real `LevelBuilder` (a few of that world's signature set-pieces on flat textured ground) and painted every frame by `LevelSelect.update()` with the game's own terrain textures (`texPattern`) + the real `physics/Obstacles` set-piece renderer, stepping a parked `PhysicsWorld` so the movers (wrecking balls, presses, compactors, fireballs, rockfalls) animate exactly as in-game — no faked CSS shapes. It's a fixed full-bleed `<canvas class="world-scene">` at z-index 0 under a `.screen-content` layer, with `#menu-root:has(.world-scene)` hiding the default grass; it needs the `Matter` global, is destroyed in `LevelSelect.exit()` (frees the physics world + resize listener), and renders a single settled frame under prefers-reduced-motion. The two Extra Modes cards use a centered flex row (`.grid.extras`) so they read large, not stranded small in the auto-fill worlds grid.

**Level creator** — `src/data/customLevels.js` + `src/screens/EditorScreen.js` + `src/screens/CustomLevelsScreen.js` ("Created Levels" card in the WorldSelect **Extra Modes** section — the concrete slab among the wooden boards; there is no Home shortcut). It's the deepest ENDGAME reward: `saveData.isCreatorUnlocked()` now requires **3 stars on every level of every playable world** (`everyLevelHasStars(3)`) and gates the card — locked/grayed with an unlock hint until then, then revealed once by the `playUnlockCutscene` reveal (see the Menu structure note below). Deep links (`?screen=editor`/`customlevels`) and the editor test pages bypass the gate on purpose (dev/test access). Player levels live in localStorage (`hillrunner_custom_v1`) as a grid: `length` columns of `CELL_W`(60)px, each cell `{ h, s?, liquid? }` where `h` is height in `UNIT_H`(25)px units above `BASE_Y`(600) and `null` = pit; obstacles are `{ type, cell, h, params }` from the `OBSTACLE_TYPES` catalog (per-type param ranges/defaults, `span(p)` footprint, `box(p)` selection extents, `pit: true` types carve the ground under themselves). Obstacles anchor at any height row (`ob.h`; older saves without it snap to the nearest ground). `compileCustomLevel()` turns that into the exact shape `LevelBuilder.finish()` emits (chain points sit at column boundaries at the *average* of neighbor heights so staircase paints compile to straight slopes; columns 0–3 and the last two are forced solid for spawn/finish; water/lava/acid runs become `water`/`molten` defs, sludge runs get their own dip chain + `sludge` def), so Terrain/Obstacles/GameScreen consume it unchanged. The editor preview is WYSIWYG: every edit recompiles and rebuilds a parked (never-stepped) `PhysicsWorld`+`Obstacles` and renders with the real set-piece renderer; the placement ghost is a second one-def parked world drawn at half alpha, and hotbar/catalog icons are the same renderer auto-cropped into data-URLs by `src/ui/ObstacleIcons.js` (its `TWEAKS` map compacts sky-high/road-wide defs for icon legibility only). Camera pans/zooms ease toward `tx/ty/tzoom` targets in `update(dt)`; drag-pan writes both for a 1:1 feel. GameScreen plays custom levels via `screens.show('game', { custom: { id, name, theme, level, from } })` — stars/best-times record under key `custom-<id>`, coins are never paid (farming), and quit/results route back to `from` ('editor' or 'customlevels'). Editor autosaves on exit and before test runs. Deep-links: `?screen=editor&id=<id>&cam=<x>`.

**Infinite mode** — `src/data/infinite.js` + `src/screens/InfiniteGameScreen.js` (extends GameScreen) + `src/screens/InfiniteSelectScreen.js` (reached from the "Infinite" card in the WorldSelect **Extra Modes** section — no longer on Home; screens `infinite`/`infinitegame`; the select screen's back button returns to `worldselect`). The Infinite card is gated by `saveData.isInfiniteModeUnlocked()` (`everyLevelHasStars(1)` — i.e. all worlds completed) and revealed once by the reveal cutscene; per-theme coin unlocks inside the select screen are unchanged. Endless per-theme runs: chunks (~2200px of "features") are generated with the SAME `LevelBuilder` the hand-made levels use (now exported from levels.js) and streamed as paired `Terrain`(+`{ startWall: false }`)/`Obstacles` instances ~4200px ahead of the car, recycled ~2400px behind (the run screen's `level.chains/walls` arrays are live — GameScreen's renderer reads them unchanged; `obstacleSet` is a facade over the chunk list). Each feature lays its own approach/hazard/recovery ground per the spacing rules above, so features never overlap. Open ground between hazards (`terrainRun`) chases a per-run coherent-noise elevation profile (`makeElevation`: multi-octave value noise, per-theme `THEME_TERRAIN` octaves/style — 'smooth' short-step chasing for Farm/Town/Mines/Castle bumpiness, 'terraced' flat-shelves-joined-by-straight-ramps for City/Factory; grades capped under the ~40° friction limit, amplitude eased in over the first ~1km, and the spawn anchor faded out over 5km so runs range BOTH high and low, ~±350px). Terrain dives deep, so `level.deathY` is dynamic (deepest live chunk + 420 — `_updateDeathY`), and a soft-lock watchdog ends the run ("Got stuck!") after 12s at rest with no forward progress — motion of any kind (reversing for a run-up, rocking loose) resets its fuse, so only a car that is genuinely parked burns it. **Fuel is the run's clock**: a tank lasts `FUEL_SECONDS` (32s), red jerry cans spawn on solid ground every `CAN_SPACING_M` (170m, placed per chunk in `_placeCans` — pits are skipped past), touching one refills the tank ("Fuel!" popup), an empty tank ends the run ("Out of gas!"). Collection is a pure distance check in `_trackFuel` — no physics bodies, so the sim never sees it. The HUD (top-left) draws the ⛽ gauge (green→amber→flashing red + a pulsing red vignette under 25%) whenever `_hudExtras` passes `fuel`. Difficulty `difficultyAt(x)` hits 1 at ~1.2km (creeps to 1.6): gaps widen (getting a wooden ramp above ~200px), press/compactor/fireball/rockfall periods shrink, etc. Per-theme feature pools in `THEME_FEATURES`. Economy: `PX_PER_M`=22; crossing a 500m milestone beyond the theme's saved best pays instantly (`milestoneCoins`, × the theme's `payMult`); flips (full airborne rotations) and air time pay at run end. Farm is free; other themes unlock with coins (`saveData.isInfiniteUnlocked`/`unlockInfinite`, best distances in `saveData.data.infinite`). GameScreen's pause-overlay/canvas handlers are guarded with `screens.current === this` because two GameScreen instances now share that DOM. Dev deep-link: `?inf=<themeId>&bot=1&warp=N`. Test page: `test-infinite.html` (generator soak across all themes + a scripted bot run asserting streaming bounds, milestone payout, retry/quit and the unlock flow). Note the Beachday font has no ∞ glyph — spell out "Infinite" in display-font strings.

Editor test pages (run like test-sim, need the local server): `test-editor-smoke.html` (compiler + every obstacle type through real physics — no localStorage), `test-editor-drive.html` (bot drives a compiled custom level to WIN), `test-editor-ui.html` (scripted mouse/DOM interaction suite; asserts land in `<pre id="testlog">`), `test-editor-shot.html` (visual states for `--screenshot`; `&cat=1` opens the obstacle catalog). The UI/shot pages overwrite the custom-level store but stash any real data in `hillrunner_custom_v1_backup` first. Headless quirk: under `--dump-dom` requestAnimationFrame never fires (only timers advance with virtual time; `--screenshot` does drive rAF) — that's why the game's own pages dump empty and the UI test waits on `setTimeout`, and why editor `update()` (dirty-rebuild) only runs under `--screenshot`.

**NEVER put ad-tech words in a filename or URL** (`ad`, `ads`, `advert`, `banner`, `sponsor`, `popup`). The break manager was originally `src/core/AdManager.js` and ad blockers refused to load it — and because a blocked import kills the whole ES module graph, `main.js` never ran and the player got a bare hillside with no menu, on a page with no ads on it. It's invisible to normal debugging: the file serves 200, `fetch()` retrieves it fine (blockers filter by *request type*), and the browser's script error event carries no detail. That's why it's now `src/core/Breaks.js`, and why `test-breaks.html` walks the module graph asserting no URL would trip a blocker. `src/boot-guard.js` also calls this out on screen.

**A dead module graph = a bare hillside, so `src/boot-guard.js` exists** — every menu is DOM built by `main.js` and its imports, so if ANY module 404s, fails to parse, or is blocked (opening `index.html` over `file://`, where browsers refuse ES modules), the player gets sky and grass: no menu, no error. The guard is a CLASSIC script (it has to survive whatever broke the modules), loaded before `main.js` in all three `index.html` copies. It shows the `#boot-error` board (styled in `style.css`): "Serve me over HTTP" on `file://`; otherwise, if `main.js` hasn't set `window.__hillRunnerBooted` within 4 s, it re-walks the import graph with `fetch` and names the file that failed (`/src/core/Platform.js — HTTP 404`), since the browser's own error event carries no detail. `main.js` routes a rejected `boot()` to the same panel via `window.showBootError(title, html)`. **A boot failure must never be a silent blank screen.**

**Game loop** — single rAF in `src/main.js`; `GameScreen.update` runs a fixed-step accumulator at 60 Hz calling `car.update()` (input + fail checks) before each `physics.step()`. Win = chassis past `level.finishX`; fail = below `level.deathY` or `Car.update` returns `'stuck'` (inverted + genuinely at rest — speed < 0.9, near-zero spin — for a 1.5 s grace window; a flipped car still sliding or being rocked is in motion and must never fail) or `'beached'` (motionless with NO wheel contact while resting on something undrivable — a wall-top perch, a high-center — for 4 s; without it that state is a frozen run, since no wheels = no drive and the chassis touch blocks air control).

**Save data** — one JSON object under `hillrunner_save_v1` behind the `saveData` singleton: coins, per-vehicle upgrades, stars, best times. Reads/writes go through `platform.storage` (localStorage on web/Poki, the account-synced data module on CrazyGames — see Portal builds), so keep the profile ONE serializable object under ONE key. Per-vehicle `upgrades` is the EQUIPPED tier per stat (what physics runs with); `ownedUpgrades` is the highest tier bought — buying is sequential, but any owned tier can be re-equipped from the upgrade screen (no sell-back). Level N unlocks when level N−1 has ≥1 star. New fields must merge over defaults in `load()` so old saves survive. `SaveData` loads once at import (so the test harnesses get a profile) and `main.js` loads it AGAIN after `await platform.init()` — that second load is the one that sees a portal's cloud save.

**Portal builds (CrazyGames / Poki)** — everything portal-specific hides behind `src/core/Platform.js`, whose base version is a no-op with localStorage storage, so the plain web game runs exactly as before. `tools/build-platforms.ps1` copies the game into `builds/crazygames/` and `builds/poki/` (gitignored, `-Zip` for upload-ready archives, dev/test files stripped) and swaps exactly TWO files per build: `index.html` (adds the portal's SDK script) and `src/core/Platform.js` (its SDK guts), both from `tools/platform/<name>/`. **So: never edit `builds/` — fix the base game and re-run the script.** The seam is `init/storage/loadingStart/loadingStop/gameplayStart/gameplayStop/happytime/showAd`; `gameplayStart/Stop` are collapsed behind an `_inGameplay` guard because portals reject duplicate/consecutive events and the game fires them from many paths (enter, restart, pause, resume, win, fail, quit), and `showAd()` must never reject — an adblocked or hung ad resolves false so the player still gets their level. `src/core/Breaks.js` (`ads`) owns the cadence: an interstitial every 3–5 levels, counter advanced ONLY by a win (`noteLevelWon()` in `_win`/`_endRun` — wrecks and retries never march the player toward an ad), taken at the next natural break via `ads.gate(fn)`, which wraps every path back into gameplay (`GameScreen.restart()`, the win screen's Next, the level/theme pickers). During a break `ads.blocking` freezes the rAF loop in `main.js` and `src/ui/AudioBus.js` suspends both AudioContexts (portals require the game paused and silent). `AudioBus` is the ONE audio gate: three independent silencers — ad break, hidden tab (`visibilitychange`; portals reject games that keep sounding in a background tab), and the player's persisted mute toggle (`saveData.isMuted()`, surfaced on the home screen and the pause overlay's `#btn-mute`, wired once in `main.js` — test pages boot `main.js` against minimal DOM, so that wiring is null-guarded) — and any one of them suspends both contexts; `Sound`/`Music` consult `isSilenced()` before creating or auto-resuming a context. `main.js` also auto-pauses an in-progress run when the tab hides. Custom/editor levels are exempt from both counter and gate. Test page: `test-breaks.html` (stubs `platform.showAd` and asserts the cadence, the wreck-retry exemption, the freeze/release, adblock recovery, and that no module URL is blocker-bait).

## Physics conventions (the part that will bite you)

- Matter stores velocity in px/step and angular velocity in **radians per 16.6 ms step**, not per second. Vehicle stats are authored in rad/s and converted with `PER_STEP = 1/60` inside `Car.js`. Speeds read off bodies are per-step.
- Screen coords are y-down, so positive angle = clockwise = nose-down for a right-facing car. Gas in air = nose up = **negative** angular velocity.
- Wheel spin alone barely moves the car (the friction solver eats small per-step increments) — propulsion is `Body.applyForce` on the chassis scaled by engine torque, with wheel spin-up layered on for feel/slip. Slopes steeper than ~40° are unclimbable (friction limit); level geometry must respect that.
- Suspension = two spring constraints per wheel anchored at the **chassis top edge**. Anchoring lower makes the triangle bistable — wheels flip above the chassis on hard landings. Don't "simplify" the anchor points.
- Grounded state comes from collision events (`PhysicsWorld` stamps `body.plugin.lastContact`), not raycasts. A wheel counts as grounded for 90 ms after its last terrain contact.
- Terrain chains become thin rotated static rectangles offset half a thickness below the surface line; walls/beams are plain static rects. Every drivable surface — including *dynamic* obstacle planks (seesaw, rope bridge) — is labeled `'terrain'`, because grounded/stuck detection keys on that label.
- Seesaw planks must spawn pre-tilted with the approach end on the ground (Obstacles.js does this); a horizontal plank presents its edge at bumper height and hard-stops the car.
- Tree canopies are `isSensor` bodies labeled `'canopy'`; `Car.update` bleeds ~10%/step of velocity while overlapping one and suppresses the stuck-fail timer (a snagged car always drops out). Place canopies so the drop-out point is solid ground, not a pit — snags should slow, not kill.
- Sensor zones generalize the canopy trick: `PhysicsWorld` stamps `plugin.last<Zone>` + `plugin.zone<Zone>` on wheels/chassis for labels `canopy`/`oil`/`updraft`/`water`; `Car.update` applies the effect (oil = signed horizontal shove from the zone's `plugin.push`; updraft = lift capped at a ~3.5 px/step terminal rise rate and tilted 30% forward — both caps prevent a slow car being tossed sky-high or hovering forever; water = heavy drag, then a `'sank'` verdict after 0.55 s submerged, so fast skims survive).
- Ballistic arcs bleed forward speed fast (chassis `frictionAir` 0.01/step ⇒ ~2 s airborne loses ~2/3 of vx), so a car launched too high lands nearly vertically. Never stack a ramp AND a fan on the same gap, and keep water/pits out of the far landing zone of any big-air feature; conversely a fan inside a gap is a good rescue for undershot jumps since the cap makes it gentle.
- Wrecking balls (and Castle's short-chain `spikyBall()` flails) are free pendulums (dense circle + stiff cable constraint, no damping) labeled `'ball'` — not terrain, so they knock the car without counting as ground. Keep other set-pieces out of the swing arc (±len·sin(angle0) around the anchor). Lethality is DIRECTIONAL (`ballStrike` in PhysicsWorld): a ball kills only when its own velocity toward the car exceeds 4 px/step; ramming a slow ball, or catching up to one swinging away, is a heavy shove instead. This is deliberate — a car crossing behind a swing accelerates faster than the ball and would otherwise die rear-ending it.
- Castle hazards: `fireballPit()` is a `moltenPit` plus a lethal-in-flight sensor fireball (label `'fireball'`, dynamic sensor so terrain never stops it; flight ≈1.9 s of the 3.6 s period — Matter's effective gravity on it is weaker than the ballistic math suggests, so measure, don't derive). `arrowVolley()` is a static sensor lethal while `plugin.raining` (mover-toggled; kill zone is 24 px narrower than the visual curtain). `spikeStrip()` is a thin sensor (label `'spikes'`) → instant `'popped'` verdict; always give it a ramp launch whose lip sits ~20 px before the strip — a crest launch LANDS on the strip. `beam()` is a dynamic terrain-labeled timber that needs a fast hit to topple and can bridge a ~140 gap when standing just before the edge.
- Castle spacing rules (same float logic as the Mines rules below): fireball pits need ~700 px of flat approach (braking must finish short of the lip — parking on it catapults the car); ramp+strip sections need ~1050 px before the next ramp (the launch float carries ~850 past the lip); keep ~1000 px between an arrow volley and the next timing hazard so backing off one doesn't reverse into the other.
- Sensor zones also cover `molten` (lava/acid: near-instant `'melted'` verdict, ~0.12 s grace for a lip graze — no water-style skimming, so `moltenPit()` resumes its far edge `dy` lower; undershooting a level exit would always graze the pool) and `bouncer` (tire stacks: `Car.update` launches the car with vy scaled by landing impact, capped at 16.5 px/step ≈ 390 px rise, tilted slightly forward, 500 ms cooldown — a stalled car can trampoline its way up, so basins are never soft-locks). Bouncer contact also counts as ground contact (you can drive on/off a stack).
- Rockfall debris is lethal only while falling (`label 'debris' && !isStatic`); parked in its chute it's scenery. Ice chains (`surface: 'ice'`) get near-zero friction — no drive, no brakes — so author them flat/downhill with speed carried in, never uphill.
- **The float artifact drives Mines-style level spacing:** any hard landing (drop, pit crossing, launch lip) can leave the car "floating" ~100–150 px above the road for ~500 px while the player (or bot) works the air-control, because pumping chassis angular velocity against the spring-mounted wheels sheds most of gravity. This is baked into the tuned feel — design around it, don't "fix" `Car.js`. Consequences: `jumpHole()` teeth sit exactly at float height, so holes need ~550+ px of clean flat approach and must never follow a landing closely; `moltenPit()` must be entered from FLAT road (an up-lip launches fast cars into a long float) and needs its landing float to settle before the next lip; a rockfall pit needs ~600 px of braking room after any jump landing (the bot brakes from `~170 + vx·30` px out, and parking on a crumble bridge or reversing back onto one kills you — keep bridges well outside that window).
- Tire-stack basins: place the stack far enough in (~320 px for a fast entry) that cars falling off the drop edge land ON it, and keep the headwall ~200 px past the stack so the first big bounce clears it; a fast entry that smacks the headwall still recovers by trampolining.
- World gating: `saveData.isWorldUnlocked(id)` requires ≥1 star on the previous world's final level; a world's first level keys off that too.

## Tuning knobs

Feel lives in three places: `engine.gravity.y` in `PhysicsWorld.js`, the stat tables in `vehicles.js`, and `DRIVE_FORCE` / spin-up multipliers in `Car.js`. These have been hand-tuned since the initial build — treat current values as intentional, and rerun `test-sim.html` after changing them (level pass/fail expectations shift with tuning).
